About UUID v4

UUID (Universally Unique Identifier) version 4 uses 122 bits of cryptographic randomness to generate a 128-bit identifier. The probability of a collision between two randomly generated UUIDs is astronomically small — roughly 1 in 5.3 × 10³⁶ per pair.

Format: xxxxxxxx-xxxx-4xxx-yxxx-xxxxxxxxxxxx

The "4" marks UUID version 4. The "y" is one of 8, 9, a, or b (variant bits).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a UUID? ▼
A Universally Unique Identifier (UUID) is a 128-bit value formatted as 8-4-4-4-12 hexadecimal digits. It is designed to be globally unique without a central coordinator.
What is UUID v4? ▼
UUID v4 is randomly generated (122 random bits). The probability of generating two identical v4 UUIDs is astronomically small — making them safe as unique IDs in databases and distributed systems.
When should I use a UUID instead of an auto-increment integer? ▼
UUIDs are better when IDs must be globally unique across systems, when you cannot predict the next ID (security), or in distributed databases where multiple nodes create records simultaneously.
