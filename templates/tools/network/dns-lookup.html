DNS Record Types Explained

A & AAAA

A maps a domain to an IPv4 address. AAAA maps to an IPv6 address. These are the most common records — they tell browsers where to send traffic.

MX

Mail Exchange records direct email for the domain to the correct mail server. Priority values determine which server to try first.

TXT

Text records store human-readable data — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC anti-spam policies, domain ownership verification, and more.

NS & CNAME

NS identifies the authoritative nameservers for the domain. CNAME creates an alias from one hostname to another (e.g. www → bare domain).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is TTL in DNS? ▼
TTL (Time-to-Live) is the number of seconds that a DNS record is cached by resolvers before they re-query the authoritative server. Lower TTLs mean faster propagation of DNS changes.
Why is this different from my local DNS? ▼
This tool queries Google's public resolver (8.8.8.8). Your local DNS may have cached older values. If you recently changed DNS records, this tool shows the globally-propagated values.
What does NXDOMAIN mean? ▼
NXDOMAIN (Non-Existent Domain) means the queried domain does not exist in DNS. It could be unregistered, expired, or have a typo in the name.
