Poetry Forms Explained

1

Sonnet

14 lines in iambic pentameter — the form Shakespeare and Keats used. Three quatrains build an argument; a final couplet delivers a twist or resolution. Best for love, beauty, and philosophical themes.

2

Haiku

Three lines of 5, 7, 5 syllables — the Japanese form focused on nature and a single moment. The best haikus contain a juxtaposition (kigo) that creates an unexpected emotional punch.

3

Free Verse

No fixed rhyme or meter — the most widely used modern form. Success depends entirely on the quality of imagery, line breaks, and sound. Harder than it looks, which is why a strong starting point matters.
