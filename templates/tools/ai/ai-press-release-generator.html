Anatomy of a Strong Press Release

1

Headline: News, not hype

The best headlines state the news directly — who did what. "Company X Launches Y" or "Company X Raises $Z" beats anything with exclamation points or superlatives.

2

Lead paragraph: answer the 5 Ws

Who, what, when, where, why — in the first two sentences. Journalists scan for this. If they can't find it immediately, they move on.

3

Quote: add the human element

A strong quote from a named executive says something a journalist can't paraphrase — an opinion, a vision, or a compelling insight. Avoid corporate non-quotes like "We're excited to announce."

Frequently Asked Questions

What is AP Style and why does it matter?
AP Style (Associated Press Stylebook) is the standard grammar and punctuation guide used by most news organizations. Writing in AP Style signals professionalism to journalists and reduces the editing they need to do — making your release more likely to be picked up as-is.
How do I distribute my press release?
For free distribution: email journalists directly (build a targeted media list), post on your newsroom/blog, and share on LinkedIn. For paid distribution: PR Newswire, Business Wire, and GlobeNewswire distribute to newsrooms. For SEO value: PRWeb and EIN Presswire are lower-cost options.
How long should a press release be?
400–600 words is the standard length. One page is ideal. If it can't fit on one page, your release either has too many topics (split it) or too much filler (cut it). Journalists get hundreds of releases a day — shorter is always better.
