The 5 Rules of High-Converting Cold Emails

1

Lead With Value, Not Self-Promotion

"I can help you [specific result]" beats "I'm a [job title] at [company]" every time. The reader's first question is: what's in it for me?

2

Keep It Under 150 Words

Decision-makers scan, they don't read. If your email can't be read in 30 seconds, it won't be read at all. Short emails get more replies.

3

One CTA Only

Give the reader one single action. "Would you be open to a 15-minute call this week?" is better than multiple asks. More options = decision paralysis.

4

Personalise the Opening Line

Reference something specific about the company or person (a LinkedIn post, recent funding, a product feature). Generic openers get ignored. Specific openers get read.

5

Follow Up (Politely)

Most replies come from the 2nd or 3rd follow-up. Send 2–3 follow-ups spaced 3–5 days apart. Each should add new value, not just "checking in."

Frequently Asked Questions

What's a good reply rate for cold emails?
Industry average is 1–5%. A well-crafted, personalised sequence can reach 10–20%. Open rates of 40–50% are achievable with strong subject lines. Track open rate (subject line test), reply rate (email body test), and meeting rate (CTA test) separately.
What's the best time to send cold emails?
Tuesday–Thursday, 7–9am or 4–6pm in the recipient's local timezone tend to perform best. Avoid Monday mornings (inbox overload) and Friday afternoons (weekend mindset). A/B test send times with your specific audience to find what works for your niche.
Should I use my personal email or a dedicated domain?
Use a dedicated subdomain (e.g., outreach@mail.yourdomain.com) to protect your main domain's email reputation. Warm up the new email address by sending low volumes for 2–4 weeks first. Never send bulk cold email from your primary business address.
