How to Build a Strong Brand Palette

1

Choose a primary color

Your primary color is the dominant brand color — the one used most and most associated with your brand. It should embody your brand personality and be memorable in isolation.

2

Add 1–2 secondary colors

Secondary colors complement the primary and provide variety without competing for attention. Use them for backgrounds, UI elements, and sections that need visual separation.

3

Include neutral tones

Whites, greys, and near-blacks anchor the palette and give visual breathing room. Most text and large background areas should use neutrals so your brand colors can pop when used.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if the colors meet WCAG accessibility standards?
The AI notes approximate contrast ratios for text usage, but for precise WCAG 2.1 AA/AAA verification, run your hex pairs through a contrast checker tool like WebAIM's Contrast Checker. For body text, aim for a 4.5:1 ratio minimum; for large headings, 3:1 is the minimum.
Can I use the generated colors in Figma or Sketch?
Yes — copy the hex codes directly into Figma, Sketch, Adobe XD, or any design tool. In Figma, paste hex codes into the color picker or add them to your Local Styles to build a design system. The RGB values are also provided for tools that use RGB input.
