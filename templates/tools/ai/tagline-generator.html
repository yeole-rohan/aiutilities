What Makes a Tagline Unforgettable?

⚡

Short enough to stick

The best taglines are 2–6 words. "Just Do It." "Think Different." "Got Milk?" Brevity forces clarity. If it takes more than 6 words, you haven't found the core idea yet.

🎯

Says something only you can say

"Quality you can trust" could be any brand. A great tagline is so specific to your brand that a competitor using it would seem odd. Own your niche, your difference, your customer's transformation.

❤️

Speaks to emotion, not features

People buy feelings, not specs. "Snap. Send. Done." beats "Share photos with friends easily." Lead with the outcome or feeling your customer wants, not the mechanism.

🔊

Sounds good out loud

Taglines are spoken in ads, presentations, and pitches. Read candidates aloud — rhythm, alliteration, and cadence matter as much as meaning. If it trips off the tongue awkwardly, it won't stick.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a tagline and a slogan?
A tagline represents the brand permanently and appears alongside the logo — it defines who you are. A slogan is campaign-specific — "I'm Lovin' It" is McDonald's tagline; a limited-time deal might have its own slogan. This tool generates taglines, but the output works for campaign slogans too.
Can I trademark the tagline this tool generates?
Potentially yes — but check with a trademark attorney first. AI-generated text is not automatically trademarked. You'll need to conduct a trademark search (via USPTO or your country's trademark office) to ensure the phrase isn't already registered in your industry class before using it commercially.
How do I know which tagline to choose?
Test them. Share your top 3 with real potential customers (not just friends and colleagues) and ask which feels most like what the brand promises. A/B test them in your marketing. The tagline that makes your target customer feel understood is the right one — not the one that sounds cleverest to you.
