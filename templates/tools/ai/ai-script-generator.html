Anatomy of a High-Retention Video Script

1

The hook (first 15 seconds)

Most viewers decide in the first 15 seconds whether to keep watching. Start with the most compelling thing — a bold claim, a surprising fact, or the payoff of watching ("by the end of this you'll know X").

2

Pattern interrupts

Change the visual or topic every 30–60 seconds. Cut to B-roll, add a graphic, ask a question. The AI includes [B-ROLL] cues to remind you where visual variety should happen.

3

One clear CTA

End with a single, clear ask — subscribe, comment, or watch the next video. Multiple CTAs dilute each other. The like-and-subscribe at the end works better after you've delivered value, not before.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many words per minute should I plan for?
The average natural speaking pace for video is 130–150 words per minute. So a 5-minute video = ~650–750 words of spoken content. Scripts are often longer than spoken output because of pauses, B-roll (where you stop talking), and on-screen text. The AI calibrates for the target duration you select.
Can I use this for TikTok or Instagram Reels?
Yes — select TikTok or Instagram Reel as the platform and choose 30–60 seconds duration. The AI adjusts the structure for short-form: faster hook, tighter content, and a simple CTA rather than a full outro.
