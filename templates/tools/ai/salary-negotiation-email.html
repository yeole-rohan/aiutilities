Salary Negotiation Tips That Work

1

Always negotiate in writing

Email gives you time to think, creates a paper trail, and removes the emotional pressure of real-time verbal negotiation. Most employers expect it and respect it.

2

Anchor high, but reasonably

Ask 10–15% above your actual target. This gives you room to meet in the middle while still landing where you want. Asking exactly at your target leaves nothing to negotiate down from.

3

Lead with value, not need

Never justify your ask with personal expenses ("my rent went up"). Frame it around market data, your accomplishments, and the value you bring. Employers pay for value, not your bills.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will negotiating hurt my job offer?
Almost never. Employers expect negotiation — it's part of the hiring process. A respectful, professional counter-offer rarely results in a rescinded offer. In fact, not negotiating signals to some employers that you undervalue yourself. The worst realistic outcome is they say no and you accept the original offer.
When should I send a salary negotiation email?
For job offers: within 24–48 hours of receiving the written offer. Don't wait past 72 hours. For raises: 2–4 weeks before your performance review, or after a major achievement. Timing matters — negotiate from a position of strength, not desperation.
What if they can't budge on base salary?
Ask what else is negotiable: signing bonus, remote work flexibility, extra PTO, accelerated review date, equity, or professional development budget. Total compensation is more than base salary — often the non-cash components have more flexibility than base pay.
