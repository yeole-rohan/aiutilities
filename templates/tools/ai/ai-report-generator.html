Report Writing Best Practices

1

Executive summary first

Most readers start — and sometimes end — at the executive summary. It should stand alone: problem, key findings, and top recommendations in under 200 words. Write it last; put it first.

2

Lead findings with insight

Don't just report data — interpret it. "Sales dropped 12%" is a finding. "Sales dropped 12% due to a 3-week inventory gap in August, not demand softening" is an insight. Reports are valuable for analysis, not just information.

3

Actionable recommendations

Every recommendation should be specific, feasible, and assigned. "Improve marketing" is advice. "Increase LinkedIn ad spend by 20% in Q1, targeting 35–54 SaaS decision-makers" is a recommendation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use this for academic reports?
This tool generates a structured framework and draft content that's useful for academic reports. For academic submission, you must verify all facts, add proper citations, and rewrite substantially in your own voice. AI-assisted drafts should be treated as starting points, not final submissions.
How do I add data and charts to the report?
Copy the report into Google Docs or Word, then insert your actual data, tables, and charts. The AI generates the narrative structure and analysis framework — your real numbers and visuals make it authoritative. Placeholder text like "[INSERT DATA]" marks where your specific figures should go.
