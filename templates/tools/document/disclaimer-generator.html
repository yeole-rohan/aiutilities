Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a disclaimer on my website?
A disclaimer is strongly recommended for most websites, especially those sharing information (health, finance, legal, fitness) or containing affiliate links. It protects you by clearly stating the limits of your liability and that your content isn't professional advice.
What's the difference between a disclaimer and a terms of service?
A disclaimer is a statement that limits your liability and clarifies what your content is NOT (e.g., not medical advice). Terms of service is a contract between you and your users governing how they may use your platform. Most sites need both.
Does an affiliate disclaimer need to be on every page?
Per FTC guidelines, affiliate disclosures must be clearly visible near the affiliate content — ideally at the top of every page or post that contains affiliate links. A footer-only disclaimer is generally not sufficient. The FTC requires disclosure to be "clear and conspicuous."
Is a disclaimer legally enforceable?
A well-written disclaimer adds meaningful legal protection, especially for negligence and misrepresentation claims. Courts generally uphold reasonable disclaimers. However, they cannot protect against fraud, intentional misrepresentation, or gross negligence.
