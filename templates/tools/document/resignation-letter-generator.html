The 5 Rules of a Great Resignation Letter

1

Be Clear About Your Last Day

State your exact last working day. Ambiguity causes awkward situations. Standard notice is 2 weeks, but check your contract.

2

Keep it Short

A resignation letter should be 3 short paragraphs. This is not the place for long explanations or complaints.

3

Express Gratitude

Thank your employer for the opportunity. Even if your experience was mixed, gratitude protects your professional reputation and future references.

4

Offer to Help with Transition

Offer to train a replacement or document your work. You don't have to go above and beyond — the gesture is what matters.

5

Never Burn Bridges in Writing

Your resignation letter may be kept in HR files indefinitely. Keep it positive — vent privately if needed, but never in the letter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to give a reason for resigning?
No. You are not legally required to state a reason. If you'd prefer not to, leave the reason field blank and the letter will omit it gracefully. If you do give a reason, keep it positive ("pursuing a new opportunity" rather than complaints).
Should I send the letter by email or in person?
Best practice is to tell your manager in person or by video call first, then send the formal letter by email (or hand it in person). Never quit by text or without a formal letter — it reflects poorly on you professionally.
How much notice should I give?
Check your employment contract first — it specifies minimum notice. Standard is 2 weeks in the US. In the UK, India, and many EU countries it may be 1–3 months. Giving more notice than required is always appreciated.
