What Makes a LinkedIn About Section Work

1

Hook in the first 2 lines

LinkedIn shows only 2–3 lines before "See more." Put your most compelling hook first — a bold claim, a key result, or a clear statement of what you do and for whom.

2

Lead with value, not history

Your About section isn't a resume timeline. Focus on what you bring — outcomes you create, problems you solve — not where you've been. Save the chronology for the Experience section.

3

End with a call to action

Tell readers what to do next: "Connect if you're building in fintech," "DM me about consulting engagements," or "Open to leadership roles in EdTech." Clear CTAs get more profile actions.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a LinkedIn About section be?
LinkedIn allows up to 2,600 characters. The sweet spot is 200–400 words — long enough to showcase your value, short enough that people read it all. Use short paragraphs and line breaks for readability. Walls of text get skipped.
Should I write in first person or third person?
First person ("I help companies...") is almost always better for LinkedIn. It feels authentic and direct. Third person ("Jane is a senior PM who...") feels formal and distant — like reading a bio someone else wrote about you. Reserve third person for speaker bios and press kits.
Will recruiters actually read my About section?
Yes — if your headline and profile photo get them to click. Recruiters check your About section to understand your trajectory and goals, which your job titles alone don't show. A strong About section also helps LinkedIn's algorithm surface you in relevant searches.
