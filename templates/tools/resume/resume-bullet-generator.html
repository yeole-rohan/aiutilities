Anatomy of a Great Resume Bullet

WEAK BULLET ✗

Responsible for managing the company's social media accounts.

STRONG BULLET ✓

Grew Instagram engagement by 47% and LinkedIn following by 3,200 in 6 months by implementing a data-driven content calendar and A/B-tested posting schedule.

✓ Start with a strong action verb (Led, Built, Grew, Reduced)
✓ Include at least one number or percentage
✓ Show the result, not just the task
✓ Keep it under 2 lines (1.5 lines is ideal)
✓ Use keywords from the job posting

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bullet points should each job have?
3–5 bullets for most positions. For your most recent or most relevant role, up to 6–7 is acceptable. Older positions should have 2–3. Quality beats quantity — every bullet should demonstrate value, not just list duties.
What if I don't have numbers or metrics for my results?
Try to estimate: "reduced processing time by approximately 30%," "managed a team of 5," "onboarded 20+ clients." Even rough figures are better than none. If truly no numbers exist, focus on scope and impact: "streamlined process for a 50-person department."
Should I use the same bullets for every application?
Ideally no — prioritize and slightly reorder bullets to match what each job posting emphasizes. If the posting mentions "cross-team collaboration," lead with bullets demonstrating that. ATS systems score resumes based on keyword density relative to the job description.
